What changed, and why it matters
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  OLD: one CI over 40 (cohort x masking-rate) cells, pooled across rates.
  NEW: per masking rate, the network-minus-control difference is formed inside
       each cohort, then a 95% t interval across the 8 cohorts.

  The old interval was too narrow for a reason worth naming: masking rates within
  a cohort share that cohort's samples, its labels and its trained weights, and
  the gene subsets are nested draws over the same 72 genes. Five rates from one
  cohort are five re-measurements, not five independent observations. Pooling them
  inflated n from 8 to 40 and shrank every interval by roughly sqrt(5).

  cells: 720  conditions: ['coexpr_graph', 'e2e_graph', 'random_graph', 'real_graph', 'shuffled_graph']
  cohorts: 8  rates: [np.float64(0.0), np.float64(0.1), np.float64(0.2), np.float64(0.3), np.float64(0.4), np.float64(0.5)]

The answer to 'which one does fig18 plot?'
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  fig18 in make_figures.py already filters to level == 'architecture_family',
  falling back to 'variant_group' only if that level is absent. It is not absent,
  so fig18 has been plotting the FAMILY level all along -- the caption was right.

  The real inconsistency is finer: it plots the saved `between` row, which is
  PAIR-WEIGHTED, while a claim about 'linear vs tree vs GAT' is a statement about
  the three family BLOCKS. With 9 GAT, 11 tree and 3 linear models the cross-family
  pairs split 99 / 33 / 27, so the pair-weighted mean is 62% the gat-vs-tree block.

  between-FAMILY (linear vs tree vs GAT), averaged over the 3 family blocks:
      Jaccard 0.4386  vs chance 0.1657  excess +0.2729
      pair-weighted equivalent: 0.4407 (difference -0.0021)

  between-VARIANT-GROUP (feature view / ablation / corruption groups):
      Jaccard 0.5003 block-averaged, 0.4976 pair-weighted

  The variant-group value is the HIGHER of the two, and necessarily so: its
  'between' set still contains many GAT-vs-GAT comparisons (a graph-corrupted
  model against a feature-ablated model sits in different variant groups but the
  same architecture family). Quoting it as evidence that architecturally
  different models fail together would overstate the case. The family-level
  block-averaged number is the defensible one, and fig18 now uses it.

  fig18 is regenerated with the block-averaged family values, the three individual
  block values overlaid as points so their spread is visible, and the pair-weighted
  and variant-group numbers annotated for comparison.

TASK 4 -- label-noise ceiling from published inter-rater kappa
==============================================================================
Why the previous ceiling was replaced
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The old figure inverted an OBSERVED AUC (0.902) to a latent noise sigma (0.663)
  and called the result a ceiling. That is circular twice over. First, it assumes
  the best model already sits AT the ceiling -- which is the proposition the
  ceiling argument exists to test, so the conclusion is smuggled into the premise.
  Second, 0.902 was not even a cross-cohort number: it is panel72_combined__logreg
  on GSE130970 alone (see figures/diagnosis.txt), and that model's true 8-cohort
  mean is 0.838. An inversion anchored on the single most favourable cohort
  produces a ceiling that flatters whatever is compared against it.

  The replacement runs in the opposite direction: published inter-rater agreement
  for fibrosis staging goes IN, and the ceiling comes OUT. No observed AUC of ours
  appears anywhere in the calculation.

The model, stated in full so it can be disagreed with
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Each patient has a latent fibrosis severity z ~ N(0, 1).
  2. A pathologist reads the biopsy and observes z + e, e ~ N(0, sigma^2),
     independently for each reader. sigma is the only free parameter.
  3. Both an ideal reader (reading z) and a real reader (reading z + e) assign
     F0-F4 using the cut points that reproduce the OBSERVED stage marginal on
     their own scale, so the stage distribution stays fixed as sigma varies.
  4. sigma is chosen so that two independent readers reproduce the published
     weighted kappa. This is done exactly, from the bivariate-normal stage table,
     not by simulation.
  5. Ceiling AUC is then the AUC of a model that recovers z PERFECTLY, scored
     against one reader's F2+ call -- the label our models are trained on.

  Assumptions that matter: normal latent severity, additive homoscedastic reader
  noise, and thresholds shared across readers. Real staging noise is probably
  worse at the middle stages than at F0 or F4, which this model does not capture.
  It is a first-order estimate and should be quoted as a range, not a line.

VERDICT (Task 4)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Implied ceiling AUC across kappa = [0.47, 0.53, 0.59] and both weightings: 0.840 to 0.942
  Implied F2+ misassignment rate: 13.7% to 23.9%

  Quadratic weighting always implies MORE noise than linear at the same kappa,
  because quadratic weights forgive one-stage disagreements almost entirely; so
  reproducing a given kappa under quadratic weighting requires a larger sigma and
  yields a lower ceiling. The two weightings bracket the honest range, which is
  why both are reported rather than one being chosen.

  figures/ currently shades a ceiling band of 0.87-0.9, which
  came from the discarded AUC inversion. This simulation does not support that band.
  The band is NOT changed by this script -- CEILING lives in make_figures.py and
  editing it would silently move a reference line in figures 4 and 5. Change it
  deliberately, or drop the band and quote the range in the caption instead.
